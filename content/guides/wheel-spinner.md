A spinner wheel is the quickest way to make a choice that everyone in the room can see is fair. You type the options, spin, and the result lands where people can watch it. The key decision for most uses is whether to remove each winner before the next spin, which turns a lottery into a fair rotation where everyone gets a turn.

Here are seven setups that work well, with the probability behind each one.

## 1. Cold-calling students without favorites

Teachers use name wheels so that questions don't always go to the same raised hands. Paste the class list, one name per line, and spin.

The detail that matters is **removing each student after they're picked**. Without removal, every spin is independent. In a class of 6, the chance that a particular student is _never_ chosen across 6 spins is (5/6)⁶, about 33%. With removal, everyone is picked exactly once in 6 spins, in a random order. Use **Remove "[name]" and spin again** after each pick.

## 2. Running a giveaway draw

For a social media or newsletter giveaway, put one entry per line, export your list, paste it in and spin with your audience watching (screen-share or record it). The visible spin builds trust in a way that "we picked a winner" doesn't.

Before you spin:

- Remove duplicate entries if the rules say one entry per person. The [Remove Duplicate Lines](/online-tool/text-tools/remove-duplicate-lines) tool can clean a pasted list first.
- Decide in advance how many winners there are, and remove each winner before drawing the next.
- The wheel holds up to 200 entries. For larger draws, a [Random Number Generator](/online-tool/security-generators/random-number-generator) over numbered entries is easier to read.

## 3. Weighted choices, like "where should we eat?"

Every line is one equal slice, so the way to give an option more chance is to add it more than once. With five lines, Pizza, Pizza, Sushi, Tacos and Salad, Pizza has a 2 in 5 (40%) chance and each of the others has 20%.

This also works for raffle tickets: someone with three tickets gets three lines.

## 4. Fair chore and task rotas

Households and teams can use the wheel to hand out unpopular tasks. List the people, spin for each chore, and remove the person once they've been assigned something. Everyone gets one task before anyone gets two. Or list the chores instead, and let each person spin for theirs.

## 5. Choosing presentation order

Who presents first in a meeting or class? Use **Shuffle** to put the whole list into a random order in one click, then read it from top to bottom. Spinning repeatedly with removal gives the same kind of result with more suspense.

## 6. Breaking decision paralysis

When two or three options are genuinely close, like which book to read next or which task to start, letting a wheel choose saves time. A useful side effect: if you feel disappointed by the result, that tells you which option you actually wanted.

## 7. Games and icebreakers

Put questions, dares, categories or team names on the wheel. Short labels work best. Entries longer than 16 characters are shortened on the wheel itself (the full text still shows as the winner), and the text gets smaller as you add more slices.

## Is the wheel actually fair?

Yes, and the reason is worth knowing. The winner is chosen _before_ the wheel starts turning, using your browser's cryptographic random number generator with unbiased sampling. The animation is then calculated to land on that slice. That means:

- Every line has exactly the same chance, wherever it sits on the wheel and however long its text is.
- How hard or when you click makes no difference.
- Sorting or shuffling the list doesn't change anyone's odds.

If you've set your device to reduce motion, the wheel skips the long spin and shows the result straight away. The odds are the same.

## Setting up a spin

On the [Wheel Spinner](/online-tool/security-generators/wheel-spinner):

1. Type or paste entries into the **Entries** box, one per line. The count updates as you type. You need at least two.
2. Optionally click **Shuffle** or **Sort** to reorder the list.
3. Click **Spin (space)**, or press the space bar when you're not typing in the box.
4. After the winner appears, click **Remove "[name]"** to take them out, then spin again for the next pick.
5. The last ten results appear under **Previous winners**.

Your list is saved in this browser only, so the same wheel is waiting next time you open the page on this device. It isn't uploaded anywhere.

## FAQ

### Can I undo removing a winner?

There's no undo button, but the name stays in **Previous winners**, so you can type it back into the list.

### Why can't I spin?

The wheel needs at least two entries. Blank lines don't count.

### Does the order of names affect the result?

No. Each line is equally likely, and the winner is picked independently of where its slice sits.
