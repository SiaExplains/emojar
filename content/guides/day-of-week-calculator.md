A day of the week calculator tells you which weekday any date falls on, past or future. Pick a date and you get the weekday instantly, along with the day of the year, the ISO week number, the quarter, whether the year is a leap year and how many days away it is. Here are seven situations where that helps, with real examples.

## 1. Which day will my birthday fall on next year?

A date moves forward one weekday each year, because 365 days is 52 weeks plus one day. It jumps two weekdays when 29 February falls in between. The table "The same date in other years" shows this for three years back and seven years ahead, so you can see at a glance which year puts your birthday on a Saturday.

**Example:** 20 July 1969, the day of the first Moon landing, was a Sunday. The table shows 1970 as Monday and 1971 as Tuesday, then 1972 jumps straight to Thursday because 1972 was a leap year and 29 February came before July.

## 2. Is the deadline on a weekend?

Contracts and forms often give a fixed date without the weekday. If it lands on a Saturday or Sunday, you know to aim for the Friday before. To count only business days between two dates, use the [working days calculator](/online-tool/date-time-tools/working-days-calculator), which skips weekends for you.

## 3. Which week number goes on the report?

Many European companies plan in ISO weeks, and the week number is not always obvious near New Year. The calculator shows it under "ISO week," and adds the week-year when it differs from the calendar year.

**Example:** 1 January 2000 was a Saturday, and its ISO week reads "Week 52 of 1999." ISO weeks start on Monday, and week 1 is the week containing the year's first Thursday, so those first two days of January 2000 still belonged to the last week of 1999. Similarly, 1 January 2027 is a Friday in "Week 53 of 2026."

## 4. How far into the year are we?

"Day of the year" shows the date's position, such as 281 of 365 for 8 October 2026. That's useful for progress tracking ("we're 77 % through the year") and day-numbered logs. The "Quarter" line also tells you instantly which financial quarter a date belongs to: 8 October is Q4.

## 5. Planning an event years ahead

If you want a Saturday in early June 2029, enter a June date and read the table for 2029, or try a few dates until the weekday lands where you want it. The "Relative to today" line also shows how far away it is, for example "In 84 days" for 31 December 2026 when checked on 8 October 2026.

## 6. Checking dates in old documents and family history

Genealogists and history readers often meet dates like "Thursday the 14th of September 1752." The calculator confirms that 14 September 1752 was a Thursday. It was the first day of the Gregorian calendar in Britain and its colonies, which skipped from 2 to 14 September that year.

One caution: the tool uses the Gregorian calendar for every date, extended backwards. For a date written before a country switched from the Julian calendar (1582 in much of Catholic Europe, 1752 in Britain), the record may name a different weekday than the calculator. That's because the writer used the old calendar, not because either is wrong.

## 7. Handling 29 February birthdays and leap years

Leap years follow a three-part rule: divisible by 4, except century years, which must be divisible by 400. So 2000 was a leap year and 1900 was not. The line "Is 2024 a leap year?" answers it directly for whichever year you pick.

**Example:** 29 February 2024 was a Thursday. In the comparison table, years without that date show "— (no 29 February)" instead of quietly switching to 28 February or 1 March. The next real one, 29 February 2028, is a Tuesday. The calculator doesn't guess which day a leap-day birthday moves to.

## Can you work it out in your head?

Roughly, yes. Start from a year you know and add one weekday per year, plus one extra for each 29 February you pass. If your birthday was a Monday this year and no leap day falls in between, next year it's a Tuesday. This is exactly the pattern the table shows. For dates decades away, the calculator is faster and handles the century-year exception.

## FAQ

### Does the calculator work for dates far in the future?

Yes. Any date your browser's date picker accepts works the same way, and the leap-year rule is applied every time, including the century exceptions such as 2100, which is not a leap year.

### Why does the ISO week sometimes show a different year?

Because ISO weeks belong to the year that contains their Thursday. Dates in the first few days of January can sit in week 52 or 53 of the previous year, and late December dates can fall in week 1 of the next year.
