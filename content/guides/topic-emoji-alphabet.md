There is no complete emoji alphabet, but you can spell almost anything with three tricks: **regional indicator letters** (🇦 to 🇿), the handful of **letter buttons** such as 🅰️ 🅱️ 🅾️ 🅿️, and **look-alike emojis** whose shape resembles a letter, like ⚡ for Z or 〽️ for M. Emojar's [letter-shaped emoji table](/table/emoji-alphabet-shaped) shows all three for every letter from A to Z, ready to copy.

## Regional indicator letters

Unicode has 26 characters called regional indicator symbols, one for each letter of the Latin alphabet, from 🇦 (U+1F1E6) to 🇿 (U+1F1FF). On their own they look like a letter in a box. They exist for one job: two of them in a row form a **country flag**. 🇫 followed by 🇷 is shown as 🇫🇷, because FR is the ISO code for France.

That is both their strength and their trap. Spell a word with regional indicators and some pairs will merge into flags: "BE" becomes 🇧🇪 (Belgium), "IT" becomes 🇮🇹 and "US" becomes 🇺🇸. The fix is to put a space — or an invisible zero-width space — between the letters so the system can't pair them. Windows doesn't draw flags at all, so there the letters stay letters, while phones merge every valid pair.

## Letter buttons: 🅰️ 🅱️ 🅾️ 🅿️ and friends

A few letters exist as real emoji "buttons", mostly for practical reasons:

- 🅰️ 🅱️ 🆎 🅾️ — blood types A, B, AB and O, from Japanese medical and dating culture.
- 🅿️ — parking.
- Ⓜ️ — a circled M, used for metro and subway signs.
- ℹ️ — information.
- ©️ ®️ ™️ — copyright, registered and trademark signs, which double as C, R and TM.

Squared words such as 🆗 🆕 🆓 🆒 🆙 and 🆘 are useful for short labels too. Symbols like 🔤 (input Latin letters), 🔠 (uppercase) and 🔡 (lowercase) show an "ABC" picture but can't spell anything else.

## Why emoji letters are bad for accessibility

Screen readers read each emoji by its name. A word spelled with look-alikes is read as a string of unrelated objects: ⚡🅾️🅾️ becomes "high voltage, O button blood type, O button blood type". Regional indicators are read as "regional indicator symbol letter A" and so on. Search engines don't treat them as letters either, so a heading written this way can't be found by its word.

Use emoji letters for decoration around real text, not instead of it: "🎉 Party tonight 🎉" is readable; a word spelled entirely in squares and buttons is not. If you want styled letters that still behave like text in bios and posts, Unicode "fancy text" (bold, script, circled letters) is closer — but it has the same screen-reader problem, so keep it short.

## Practical tips

- **Spelling with regional indicators:** separate the letters with spaces, or check the result on a phone before posting — accidental flags are easy to miss on a PC.
- **Copying:** click any emoji in the [letter-shaped table](/table/emoji-alphabet-shaped) to copy it; the regional indicator for each letter is listed next to the look-alikes.
- **Designs and slides:** for crisp letters in an image, download the emojis with [Emoji to PNG](/emoji-to-png) instead of screenshotting text.
- **Codes:** paste any emoji into the [Emoji Inspector](/emoji-inspector) to see its codepoints — useful to check whether a "letter" is a regional indicator, a button or something else entirely.

## Common questions

**Is there an emoji for every letter?** As a regional indicator, yes — all 26. As a dedicated button, only A, B, O, P and M (plus AB). Everything else is a look-alike.

**Why did my word turn into a flag?** Two regional indicator letters next to each other form a flag if they match a country code. Add a space between them.

**Can I use emoji letters in a username?** Many sites accept them, but some strip or block them, and they make your name hard to search for or read aloud.
