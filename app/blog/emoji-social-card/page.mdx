Visual cards perform better on social media than plain text posts — they stop the scroll. The [Emoji Social Card Generator](/emoji-card) on Emojar lets you build a clean, shareable card in under a minute and download it as a PNG ready to post anywhere.

## What You Can Build

The generator gives you a 1200×630 canvas — the standard Open Graph image size, which works perfectly for Twitter/X cards, LinkedIn posts, and Instagram stories when cropped. You control:

- **Emoji** — the central visual, displayed large at the top of the card
- **Headline** — your main text, displayed bold below the emoji
- **Subtext** — an optional second line for context, a URL, or a tagline
- **Gradient background** — choose from preset color gradients or pick your own start and end colors
- **Font size** — adjust to fit longer or shorter text

## How to Use It

Open the [Emoji Social Card](/emoji-card) tool, type your headline, pick an emoji to feature, choose a gradient that fits your vibe, and optionally add a subtext line. The preview updates live. When it looks right, hit **Download PNG** and the card saves directly to your device.

No account required. No watermark added. Your card, your design.

## Use Cases

**Announcement posts**: Launching something? A card with a relevant emoji and a short headline is far more shareable than text alone.

**Quote cards**: Pair a meaningful quote with an emoji that captures its mood — great for Instagram or LinkedIn.

**Event promotions**: Create a simple card with the event emoji, date, and a short description for quick social sharing.

**Product updates**: Use a tool or feature emoji alongside a one-line update to make release notes visually engaging.

**Personal branding**: Build a consistent visual style using a signature emoji across all your social posts.

## Tips

Keep headlines short — under 8 words renders cleanly on all platforms. For Twitter/X, the card renders at 1200×630 and displays full-width in the feed. For Instagram, square crop works best. Use a gradient that contrasts clearly with white text — dark blues, deep purples, and forest greens tend to be most readable.
