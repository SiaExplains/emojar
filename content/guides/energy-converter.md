Energy units all measure the same thing, the capacity to do work, so they convert with fixed factors. The ones you'll need most: **1 kcal (a food Calorie) = 4.184 kJ**, **1 kWh = 3.6 MJ = 3,412 BTU**, and **1 Wh = 3,600 J**. Food labels, power bills, heating systems and batteries simply grew up with different units.

Here are six everyday places where energy conversions come up, with the numbers worked out.

## The core conversion factors

| Unit                       | In joules       |
| -------------------------- | --------------- |
| Calorie, small (cal)       | 4.184           |
| Kilocalorie (kcal)         | 4,184           |
| Watt-hour (Wh)             | 3,600           |
| Kilowatt-hour (kWh)        | 3,600,000       |
| British thermal unit (BTU) | ≈ 1,055.06      |
| Foot-pound (ft·lbf)        | ≈ 1.356         |
| Electronvolt (eV)          | ≈ 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ |

## 1. Reading food labels

Nutrition labels in the US list "Calories" with a capital C, which are **kilocalories**. Labels in Europe and Australia often list kilojoules first.

- A 250 kcal snack is **1,046 kJ**.
- An 840 kJ portion is about **200.8 kcal**.
- A 2,000 kcal daily reference intake is **8,368 kJ**.

A quick estimate: kJ ÷ 4 is a little over the kcal figure; kcal × 4.2 is close to kJ.

**Watch out:** the small calorie (cal) is a thousand times smaller than a food Calorie. In the converter, choose **Kilocalorie (kcal)** for anything from a nutrition label, not "Calorie (cal)". To estimate how many kilocalories you burn a day, the [BMR Calculator](/online-tool/calculators/bmr-calculator) gives a starting figure.

## 2. Understanding your electricity bill

Electricity is billed in **kilowatt-hours**: the energy used by a 1-kilowatt appliance running for an hour. A 2 kW heater left on for 3 hours uses 6 kWh.

In other units, 1 kWh is:

- **3,600 kJ** (3.6 MJ)
- **860.4 kcal**
- **3,412.1 BTU**

So 3 kWh, a modest amount of household use, holds as much energy as about **2,581 kcal**, more than a day's food for many adults.

## 3. Sizing an air conditioner or heater

Air conditioners in the US are rated in **BTU per hour**. That is a power rating, but converting the energy unit gives you the electrical-style figure: 12,000 BTU = **3.517 kWh**, so a "12,000 BTU" unit delivers about **3.5 kW** of cooling. (Its electricity use is lower, since air conditioners move more heat than they consume.)

## 4. Comparing batteries

Laptop and power-bank batteries are often rated in watt-hours. A 50 Wh battery stores **180 kJ**. For comparison, 1 kcal is about **1.16 Wh**, so a 50 Wh battery holds roughly as much energy as 43 food Calories. It puts into perspective how energy-dense food is.

## 5. Physics and chemistry homework

Atomic and particle physics use the **electronvolt**, the energy an electron gains crossing one volt: 1 eV ≈ **1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J**. The converter shows very small results like this in scientific notation, such as 1.60217663e-19.

## 6. Mechanical work in imperial units

Engineers working in imperial units use the **foot-pound** (ft·lbf), the work done lifting one pound by one foot. 1 ft·lbf ≈ **1.356 J**, which shows up in torque wrench and impact-energy specs.

## Energy vs power: a common mix-up

**Energy** is an amount (joules, kWh, kcal). **Power** is a rate of energy use (watts, kW). A 60 W bulb uses 60 joules every second; leave it on for 10 hours and it has used 600 Wh, or 0.6 kWh. The converter works with energy amounts, so convert power ratings only after multiplying by time.

## FAQ

### Why are there different BTU values online?

There are several historical definitions of the BTU. This converter uses the International Table BTU, about 1,055.06 J. The others differ by a fraction of a percent.

### How many joules are in a food Calorie?

4,184 J, because a food Calorie is a kilocalorie.

### Can I convert kWh to liters of fuel?

Not with a unit conversion alone. That depends on the fuel's energy density, which differs between gasoline, diesel and others.
