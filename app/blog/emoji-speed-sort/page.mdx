Speed Sort is a game about fast thinking, not fast clicking. An emoji appears on screen and you have a very short window to decide which category it belongs to. Tap the wrong one or run out of time and the round is marked incorrect. Ten rounds, three seconds each — it sounds easy until a 🦋 appears and you briefly question whether it belongs in Animals or Nature.

## How a Round Works

Each of the 10 rounds follows the same structure:

1. An emoji is displayed prominently in the centre of the screen.
2. Below it, category buttons appear (for example: **Animals**, **Food**, **Nature**, **Objects**, **People**, **Travel**).
3. A 3-second timer starts immediately.
4. Tap the correct category before time runs out.

A correct answer scores a point and moves to the next round. An incorrect answer or timeout scores zero for that round but the game continues — you always complete all 10 rounds.

## The Categories

The categories used are drawn from the standard Unicode emoji groupings, so they match how you might already think about emojis:

- **Animals** — land and sea creatures, insects, mythical animals
- **Food** — fruits, vegetables, meals, drinks, desserts
- **Nature** — plants, weather, celestial objects, landscapes
- **Objects** — tools, household items, technology, clothing
- **People** — faces, gestures, family, professions
- **Travel** — vehicles, buildings, maps, flags

Some emojis sit on the obvious end of the spectrum (🐶 is Animals, 🍕 is Food) and some require a split-second judgment call (🌵 could feel like Nature or Travel depending on context, but it is Nature).

## Scoring

Your score is the count of correct answers out of 10. A perfect 10/10 is very achievable at a relaxed pace but requires genuine speed and category knowledge when you are playing for a high score.

The timer pressure is the whole mechanic — most people know perfectly well that 🐘 is an animal; the challenge is knowing it fast enough under a ticking clock.

## Tips for Faster Categorisation

**Learn the edge cases.** A few emojis feel ambiguous at first: 🌞 (Nature, not People), 🧲 (Objects), 🏖️ (Travel). Playing multiple rounds quickly exposes your personal blind spots so you can learn them.

**Don't second-guess.** Your first instinct is almost always correct. The 3-second timer is just enough for a deliberate first response but not enough for second-guessing. Commit fast.

**Watch the layout.** The category buttons do not shuffle position between rounds within a session. Once you know where "Food" lives on the screen, your eyes can anticipate the tap before your brain fully finishes processing.

**Improve by playing adjacent games.** Games like [Emoji Wordle](/games/emoji-wordle) and [Emoji Pictionary](/games/emoji-pictionary) build your general emoji-naming vocabulary, which makes categorisation faster.

## The Challenge Gets Real After a Few Games

Your first run of Speed Sort often scores 7–9 out of 10. The challenge is achieving perfect 10/10 scores consistently. Small hesitations on ambiguous emojis are what separate 9/10 from 10/10. That consistency goal is what keeps the game engaging across dozens of replays.
