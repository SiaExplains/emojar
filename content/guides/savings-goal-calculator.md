To find how much to save each month, take the amount you still need, spread it over the months you have, and let interest cover a small part of the gap. Saving 20,000 in three years when you already have 2,000 takes 500 a month with no interest, or 464.77 a month if the money earns 4% a year. The longer the timeline and the higher the rate, the more interest does for you.

Below are five common goals worked through in the Savings Goal Calculator, each showing a different way to use it. The rates are assumptions for illustration. Results are estimates, not financial advice, and savings rates change over time.

## 1. An emergency fund in two years

Many people aim to keep a few months of expenses in an easy-access account. Say that target is 15,000 and you already have 3,000 set aside in an account paying 4% a year.

- **Save each month:** 471.10
- **Total deposits:** 11,306.38
- **Interest earned:** 693.62

The **Year by year** table shows the balance reaching 8,880.22 after the first year and exactly 15,000.00 at the end of the second. Interest covers almost 700 of the 12,000 you were missing.

## 2. A car paid for in cash

You want 12,000 for a used car in three years, starting from nothing, in a savings account paying 3%.

- **Save each month:** 318.97
- **Interest earned:** 516.92

Without interest you would need 333.33 a month. Saving for a car instead of financing it also means the interest works for you rather than against you; the [Loan Calculator](/online-tool/calculators/loan-calculator) shows what the borrowing route would cost.

## 3. A home deposit with a fixed monthly budget

Here the question flips. You have 10,000 toward a 60,000 deposit, expect 4% a year, and can put away 700 a month. How long will it take? Switch **Calculate** to **Time to reach the goal** and enter 700 as the **Monthly saving**:

- **Time to goal:** 5 years 2 months
- **Total deposits:** 43,400.00
- **Interest earned:** 7,013.03

If you would rather finish in exactly five years, switch back to **Monthly saving needed** with 5 years: the answer is 720.83 a month. Comparing the two modes this way shows what an extra 20 a month buys you, which here is about two months.

Squeezing the same goal into 18 months is a different story: enter 1.5 years and the required saving jumps to 2,666.57 a month. Short deadlines leave little time for interest, so almost all the work falls on your deposits. Once you know the deposit is covered, the [Mortgage Calculator](/online-tool/calculators/mortgage-calculator) estimates the monthly cost of the home itself.

## 4. A vacation next year

Short goals usually sit in cash, so leave the rate at 0. A 4,000 trip with 500 already saved and one year to go:

- **Save each month:** 291.67
- **Total deposits:** 3,500.00

If 250 a month is all the budget allows, the time mode says 14 months. That is the useful part of running both modes: it tells you whether to trim the trip, save harder, or book a little later.

## 5. A round-number milestone

Sometimes the goal is simply a number, such as 20,000 in savings. Starting from 2,000 at 4% and saving 500 a month, the calculator reaches it in **2 years 10 months**, with 17,000.00 deposited and 1,208.71 earned in interest. Saving the required 464.77 instead takes exactly three years. Either way, the year-by-year table is a motivating thing to check against your real balance every few months.

## FAQ

### Why does the final year in the table look short?

In time mode the goal is usually reached partway through a year. The last row shows the balance at the month the goal is reached, so its deposits cover fewer than 12 months.

### Should I include interest for a goal under a year?

You can, but the effect is small. On short timelines nearly all of the money comes from deposits, so entering 0 gives a simpler and slightly more cautious plan.

### What if I can save different amounts in different months?

The calculator uses one steady monthly amount. Use your realistic average, then rerun it whenever your balance or budget changes to see the new monthly target.
