Tic-Tac-Toe is one of the oldest strategy games in the world, and the Emojar version gives it a fresh coat of emoji paint. Instead of plain Xs and Os you play with any emoji you like — and the board can grow up to 5×5 for a real challenge.

## How to Play

The rules are the classic ones:

1. Two players take turns placing their piece on an empty cell.
2. The first player to align a full row, column, or diagonal wins.
3. If all cells are filled with no winner, the game is a draw.

On Emojar you can play **against a friend** in the same browser, or challenge the **computer opponent** and try to beat its AI.

## Choosing Your Emoji Pieces

Before the game starts, each player can pick any emoji from the full Emojar library to use as their piece. Want to battle 🦊 vs 🐼? Go for it. The emoji picker includes a search bar, so finding your favourite takes only a second.

## Board Sizes

The XO game supports three board sizes:

| Size | Win condition |
| ---- | ------------- |
| 3×3  | 3 in a row    |
| 4×4  | 4 in a row    |
| 5×5  | 5 in a row    |

Larger boards mean more room to manoeuvre — and more ways for the game to end in a draw if both players play defensively.

## The Computer Opponent

The AI follows a priority strategy:

1. **Win** — if there is a winning move available, take it.
2. **Block** — if the opponent is about to win, block them.
3. **Centre** — claim the centre cell when it is free.
4. **Corner** — occupy a corner to maximise winning lines.
5. **Random** — fill any remaining open cell.

On a 3×3 board a perfect human player can always force a draw. The challenge is to see how quickly you can do it.

## Tips for Winning

- **Take the centre first** on a 3×3 board — it gives you the most winning lines.
- **Force a fork** — create two simultaneous threats so your opponent can only block one.
- On larger boards, focus on **building consecutive runs** rather than isolated placements.
- Pay close attention to diagonals; they are easy to overlook until it is too late.

## Scoreboard

Emojar tracks your **fastest wins** per board size and per mode (vs computer / vs friend). Your scores are saved locally in the browser, so you can race to beat your own record.
