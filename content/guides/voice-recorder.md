Clean voice recordings at home come down to three things: a quiet, soft-furnished room, a consistent distance from the microphone, and a level that stays strong without touching the top of the meter. Get those right and even a laptop's built-in mic sounds respectable; get them wrong and no format or bitrate will rescue the take. Use the checklist below before, during and after each recording.

## Before you record

**Pick the quietest, softest room you have.** Hard walls, bare floors and windows bounce your voice back into the mic as a hollow echo. A bedroom with a bed, curtains and a full wardrobe is usually better than a kitchen or an empty home office.

**Switch off the background noise you've stopped hearing.** Fans, air conditioning, fridges, a laptop fan spinning up and notifications all end up in the recording. Silence your phone and close apps that make sounds.

**Choose the right microphone.** If you have a headset or USB mic, use it; it sits closer to your mouth than a laptop mic. The browser asks for microphone permission the first time and uses your system's default input, so set the input you want in your operating system's sound settings first.

**Test your setup.** Run a quick check with the [Microphone Test](/online-tool/device-tests/microphone-test) to confirm the right mic is picked up and the level moves when you speak.

**Know where the recording goes.** In the browser recorder, the audio exists only in that tab's memory until you download it. Plan to save each take before closing or reloading the page.

## While you record

**Keep a steady distance of 15–30 cm (6–12 inches).** Closer gives a fuller, warmer sound but exaggerates breaths and "p" and "b" pops; farther adds room echo. A fist-and-a-half to two hand-widths away is a good starting point. Whatever you pick, keep it the same for the whole take, because moving your head changes the volume.

**Watch the level meter.** The bar shows the peak level of your voice in real time:

- **Green and moving well past the middle on normal speech:** good.
- **Barely moving:** move closer or raise the input volume in your system settings.
- **Turning red:** you're within about 10 % of the maximum. Back off a little; loud peaks that hit the ceiling distort and can't be repaired afterwards.

**Speak off-axis to tame pops.** Angle the mic slightly to the side of your mouth rather than straight in front, so bursts of air pass it.

**Use pause instead of starting over.** If a truck goes past or you lose your place, click **Pause**, wait, and click **Resume**. The recording continues in the same file, and the timer keeps counting only while you're recording.

**Leave two seconds of silence at the start and end.** It gives you room to trim cleanly and fade without cutting into words.

## A quick note on the built-in processing

The recorder asks the browser for **echo cancellation** and **noise suppression**. These help with steady background hum and with speaker sound leaking into the mic, which is ideal for voice memos and calls. They also process your voice slightly, which can make it sound a little "phone-like" in a very quiet room. The best fix is still a quiet room: the less noise there is to suppress, the more natural the result.

## After you record

**Listen back with headphones.** Laptop speakers hide hiss, echo and mouth clicks. Use the player that appears after you click **■ Stop**.

**Save in the right format for where the file is going.** The tool gives you three options:

| Button                 | What you get                            | Best for                                          |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Download (WEBM or M4A) | The original recording, unconverted     | Smallest file, quick sharing, playing in browsers |
| Save as MP3            | 128 kbps MP3, converted in your browser | Apps, email, podcast hosts that require MP3       |
| Save as WAV            | Uncompressed 16-bit WAV                 | Editing in an audio program                       |

The original format depends on your browser's recorder, typically Opus audio in WebM in Chrome and Firefox, or AAC in M4A in Safari. The download button shows the actual format and size. For comparison, a 128 kbps MP3 uses about 128,000 × 60 ÷ 8 = 960,000 bytes per minute, roughly 1 MB, while a mono WAV at 48,000 Hz uses about 5.8 MB per minute and a stereo one twice that.

**Trim the dead air.** Load the file into the [Audio Trimmer](/online-tool/audio-video-tools/audio-trimmer) to cut the silence at each end and add a short fade.

**Name and back up your takes.** Downloads are named `recording-` plus a date and time stamp. Rename the keeper to something meaningful before it gets lost among the others.

## How to record step by step

1. Open the [Voice Recorder](/online-tool/audio-video-tools/voice-recorder) and click **● Start recording**.
2. Allow microphone access when the browser asks. The timer starts and the level meter moves as you speak.
3. Use **Pause** and **Resume** as needed, then click **■ Stop**.
4. Play the take back and choose **Download**, **Save as MP3** or **Save as WAV**.

## FAQ

### Why does the browser say the microphone was blocked?

You (or a previous visit) denied permission. Click the icon in the address bar to allow the microphone for the site, then try again. If it says the mic is busy, close other apps that are using it, such as a video-call app.

### Can I record for an hour?

Yes, but the recording is kept in memory until you download it. For long sessions, stop and download in chunks so a crashed tab doesn't cost you everything.

### Does a better format fix a noisy recording?

No. WAV preserves exactly what the mic captured, noise included. Fix the room, then record again.
