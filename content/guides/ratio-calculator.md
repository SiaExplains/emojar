A ratio compares two amounts, and most practical ratio questions are one of three jobs: finding a missing amount so two ratios stay equal, reducing a ratio to its simplest form, or splitting a total in a given ratio. Picking the wrong one is how a cleaning solution ends up too strong.

The six examples below each use one of those jobs in the [Ratio Calculator](/online-tool/calculators/ratio-calculator), whose **Calculate** menu offers *Missing value (A : B = C : D)*, *Simplify a ratio* and *Split a total in a ratio*.

## 1. Stretching a sauce you already started

A glaze calls for soy sauce and honey in a 2 : 3 ratio. You have already poured 50 ml of soy sauce. How much honey goes in?

This is a missing value: 2 : 3 = 50 : ?. Enter A = 2, B = 3, C = 50 and leave **D** empty. The answer is **D = 75**, and the proportion line reads 2 : 3 = 50 : 75. It is cross-multiplication, rearranged for the blank.

## 2. Diluting a concentrate (the classic mistake)

A floor cleaner says "dilute 1 : 9" and you want a 500 ml spray bottle. There are two ways to read that, and they give different answers:

| Reading | Calculation | Concentrate | Water |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 500 ml is the **finished** bottle | Split 500 in 1 : 9 | 50 | 450 |
| 500 ml is the **water** only | 1 : 9 = ? : 500 | 55.5555555556 | 500 |

For a bottle that holds 500 ml, you want the first row: choose *Split a total in a ratio*, enter A = 1, B = 9 and **Total to split** = 500. The result is **50 and 450**, with A shown as 10 % and B as 90 %. A 1 : 9 ratio means one part in ten, not one part in nine.

The second reading is right only when the label says "per" an amount of water. For example, 1 part concentrate to 4 parts water with 750 ml of water gives 1 : 4 = ? : 750, so **C = 187.5** ml of concentrate.

## 3. Splitting rent by room size

Two roommates share an apartment for 1,800 a month. One bedroom is noticeably bigger, and you agree on a 5 : 4 split. Choose the split mode, enter A = 5, B = 4 and a total of 1,800. The shares come out as **1,000 and 800**, or 55.5556 % and 44.4444 %.

The same mode handles prize money or tip pools: splitting 45 in a 3 : 2 ratio gives **27 and 18**.

## 4. Reading a hiking map

On a 1 : 25,000 map, how far is a trail that measures 4 cm? Solve 1 : 25000 = 4 : ?. The answer is **D = 100,000**, in the same unit as C, so 100,000 cm, which is 1 km.

Scale questions are where the **1 : n form** helps. Simplify 2 : 50 and you get 1 : 25, which reads directly as "every unit on the model is 25 in real life."

## 5. Cleaning up awkward numbers

A class with 12 boys and 18 girls simplifies to **2 : 3**. Decimals are fine too: 0.25 : 0.4 becomes **5 : 8**, because the calculator scales both numbers to whole numbers first and then divides by their greatest common divisor.

The simplify mode also shows the ratio **As a fraction** (5/8), in **n : 1 form** (0.625 : 1) and as a **Percent split** (38.4615 % / 61.5385 %).

## 6. Mixing three ingredients

Concrete, mortar and some recipes use three-part ratios such as 1 : 2 : 3, and the calculator takes two parts at a time. You can still do it in two steps. For 60 kg of a 1 : 2 : 3 mix, first split 60 in 1 : 5 (the first part against the other two combined) to get **10 and 50**. Then split that 50 in 2 : 3 to get **20 and 30**. The mix is 10, 20 and 30 kg.

## When the calculator stops you

- **Fill exactly one blank.** Leaving two of A to D empty shows "Fill in three of A, B, C and D and leave one empty."
- **Zeros in the wrong place.** 0 : 4 = 3 : ? has no answer because it would require dividing by zero, so you get a message instead of a number.
- **Simplify and split need positive numbers.** A ratio like −2 : 4 is rejected in those modes.
- **Units carry through.** The answer is in whatever unit you used for the matching term, so keep C and D in the same unit.

If your ratio is really a fraction problem, such as adding 2/3 of a cup to 3/4 of a cup, the [Fraction Calculator](/online-tool/calculators/fraction-calculator) is the better tool.

## FAQ

### Is a ratio of 3 : 4 the same as 3/4?

As a comparison, yes: the first amount is three-quarters of the second. But 3 : 4 split into a whole gives 42.8571 % and 57.1429 % (three-sevenths and four-sevenths), not 75 % and 25 %. That is why the split mode shows percentages next to the shares.

### Does the order of the numbers matter?

Yes. 2 : 3 and 3 : 2 describe opposite mixes. Keep the same item in the same position on both sides of a proportion, for example soy first and honey second.
