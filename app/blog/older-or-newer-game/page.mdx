We've added a new game to Emojar: **[Older or Newer](/games/older-or-newer)**. It's a deceptively tricky quiz that tests whether you know your emoji history — specifically, which of two emojis was added to the Unicode standard first.

## How It Works

Each round shows two emojis side by side. The left emoji's Unicode version is revealed upfront. Your job is to decide whether the right emoji is **older** (added to Unicode before the left one) or **newer** (added after).

After you guess, both version numbers and release years are shown, along with a plain-English explanation of the result. Then hit **Next Round** to keep going.

The game tracks:
- **Correct answers** — your running total
- **Streak** — consecutive correct guesses
- **Best streak** — your personal record
- **Accuracy** — percentage correct overall

A **recent rounds table** at the bottom shows your last 15 picks so you can review where you went right or wrong.

Emojis on Emojar span **16 Unicode versions** released between 2010 and 2024:

| Version | Year | Notable additions |

| 1.0 | 2015 | First dedicated Emoji standard; 1,282 emojis |
| 3.0 | 2016 | 🤣 🤦 🧀 and many more |

| 15.1 | 2023 | 🫨 🍋‍🟩 new family variants |
| 16.0 | 2024 | The most recent additions |

## Why It's Harder Than It Looks

The instinct is to assume that common, simple emojis are older — but that's often wrong. Some surprisingly complex-looking emojis date back to Unicode 0.6 (2010), while certain simple icons only appeared in recent versions.

The game also includes a **difficulty hint** before you guess — 😊 Easy when the two emojis are from very different eras, 🤔 Tricky when they're a few versions apart, and 🔥 Hard when they're in adjacent versions and the margin is razor thin.
