To find a fitness starting point you can trust, take a week of morning weigh-ins and use their average, measure your neck and waist two or three times and average those too, then run the numbers through four calculators: body fat from the tape readings, an ideal weight range from your height, your resting and daily calories, and a daily calorie target for your goal. Emojar has a calculator for each step. They work out the results in your browser as you type, and nothing you enter is sent anywhere or saved. All of them are estimates for general information, not medical advice.

The example in this guide is a 34-year-old man who is 180 cm tall and trains one to three days a week. He wants to lose some fat and needs numbers to plan around, so he spends a week collecting them before changing anything.

## Why average a week of weigh-ins instead of using one?

Body weight shifts from day to day with water, salt and meal timing, so one reading on its own can mislead. His seven morning readings were 84.6, 85.1, 84.2, 84.9, 85.4, 84.4 and 84.8 kg.

Paste them into the [Average Calculator](/online-tool/calculators/average-calculator), one per line or separated by commas or spaces. It shows:

- **Average (mean)**: 84.77142857
- **Median**: 84.8
- **Minimum / maximum**: 84.2 / 85.4, so a **Range** of 1.2
- **Standard deviation (population / sample)**: 0.380655 / 0.411154

The lightest and heaviest mornings are 1.2 kg apart. He rounds the mean to 84.8 kg and uses that in every tool after this one. If you do the same each week and compare averages, a normal up-and-down week won't look like progress or a setback.

If your phone or scale writes decimals with a comma, watch out: the calculator also treats a comma as a separator between numbers. `84,6 85,1` is read as four numbers (84, 6, 85 and 1) and gives a mean of 44. Type decimals with a point.

## How do you estimate body fat with only a tape measure?

The [Body Fat Calculator](/online-tool/calculators/body-fat-calculator) uses the U.S. Navy method, which estimates body fat from height and a few circumferences. Choose **Metric (cm, kg)** or **Imperial (in, lb)** and your sex. Men enter height, neck (just below the larynx) and waist at the navel. Women also enter hips at the widest point, and measure the waist at its narrowest point.

The tool's own advice is to keep the tape level and snug without pressing into the skin, stay relaxed, and take the average of two or three readings. His three waist readings were 93.5, 94 and 93 cm, which the Average Calculator turns into 93.5. His neck came out at 39 cm each time.

With 180 cm, 39 cm and 93.5 cm entered, the result is **Body fat** 21.6 % and **Category (ACE)** Average. The **Weight (optional)** field adds two more lines; with 84.8 kg in it he gets **Fat mass** 18.28 kg and **Lean mass** 66.52 kg. A table under the result lists the ACE categories, where men from 14 to 17 % count as Fitness and 18 to 24 % as Average.

The method is typically within about 3 to 4 percentage points of a lab measurement, and it's less accurate for very muscular or very lean people. Re-measure the same way, at the same spots, every few weeks so the readings are comparable.

## What should your weight be for your height?

There's no single answer. The [Ideal Weight Calculator](/online-tool/calculators/ideal-weight-calculator) shows four formulas side by side, so you see the spread. Each one starts from a base weight at 5 feet and adds a fixed amount per extra inch. For a man of 180 cm it gives:

| Formula | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Devine | 74.99 kg |
| Robinson | 72.65 kg |
| Miller | 71.52 kg |
| Hamwi | 77.34 kg |

The headline line, **Ideal weight (formula range)**, is 71.5–77.3 kg. Below it, **Healthy BMI range (18.5–24.9)** shows 59.9–80.7 kg, the weights at which his BMI would fall between 18.5 and 24.9.

At 84.8 kg he's about 4 kg above the top of the BMI range, and the body fat result helps make sense of that. If he kept all 66.52 kg of lean mass and got down to 17 % body fat, the top of the Fitness band, he'd weigh about 66.52 ÷ 0.83 ≈ 80.1 kg. The calculator doesn't show this; it's a quick sum by hand, and it assumes he loses no muscle. Still, 80.1 kg sits just inside the BMI range and gives him a goal he can check with the tape.

The formulas take no account of muscle or frame size, and they're meant for adults. The tool only accepts heights between 120 and 230 cm (47 to 90 in). For imperial, enter total inches, so 5 ft 10 in is 70.

## How many calories do you burn in a day?

The [BMR Calculator](/online-tool/calculators/bmr-calculator) uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. Enter sex, age, weight in kg and height in cm (there's no imperial option here), then pick an activity level. His inputs are male, 34, 84.8 kg, 180 cm and **Light (1–3 days/week)**:

- **Basal metabolic rate (BMR)**: 1,808 kcal/day
- **Daily calories to maintain weight (TDEE)**: 2,486 kcal/day
- **Mild weight loss (≈ −0.25 kg/week)**: 2,211 kcal/day
- **Weight loss (≈ −0.5 kg/week)**: 1,936 kcal/day

BMR is what you burn at complete rest. TDEE multiplies it by the activity factor, which runs from 1.2 for **Sedentary** to 1.9 for **Very active**; Light is 1.375. The two weight-loss lines subtract 275 and 550 kcal from TDEE.

Be honest about activity. Choosing **Moderate (3–5 days/week)** when you train twice a week inflates the maintenance figure, and any deficit you plan from it comes out smaller in practice.

## How do you turn that into a daily calorie and protein target?

The [Calorie Calculator](/online-tool/calculators/calorie-calculator) takes the same inputs, also in kg and cm, plus a **Goal** and a **Macro split**. With **Lose 0.5 kg per week** and **Balanced (30% protein, 40% carbs, 30% fat)** he gets:

- **Daily calorie target**: 1,936 kcal/day
- **Maintenance (TDEE)**: 2,486 kcal/day
- **Protein**: 145 g/day
- **Carbohydrates**: 194 g/day
- **Fat**: 65 g/day

The target matches the BMR Calculator's 0.5 kg line because both use about 7,700 kcal per kg of body fat, which works out to 550 kcal a day for half a kilo a week. The grams come from the split, at 4 kcal per gram for protein and carbs and 9 for fat. The other splits are **Lower carb (35 / 25 / 40)** and **Higher carb (20 / 55 / 25)**.

The calculator won't set a weight-loss target below 1,200 kcal for women or 1,500 kcal for men. If your numbers fall under that, the target is raised and a note under the result says so.

## How often should you redo the numbers?

Weigh in daily if you like, but judge by the weekly average. Re-measure neck and waist every few weeks. Once the average has dropped by a few kilos, put the new figure into the BMR and Calorie Calculators. Weight is part of the equation, so a lower weight gives a lower TDEE, and the old target slowly turns into a smaller deficit. Run the Body Fat Calculator again at the same time to see whether the weight that came off was mostly fat.

## FAQ

### Can I enter pounds and inches?

In the Body Fat and Ideal Weight Calculators, yes: choose **Imperial (in, lb)**. The BMR and Calorie Calculators only take kg and cm.

### Why do the ideal weight formulas disagree?

Each was built from different data for a different job (Devine's is still used to dose drugs), so the constants differ. Use the four results together as a rough range.

### Is body fat percentage better than BMI?

It says more about what your weight is made of: two people with the same BMI can carry very different amounts of fat. Both are rough screening numbers, and neither is a diagnosis.

### Are my measurements stored anywhere?

No. These calculators run in your browser, and the numbers you type aren't uploaded or saved.
