Most Roman numeral mistakes come from subtracting the wrong way. Only six subtractive pairs are standard, IV, IX, XL, XC, CD and CM, so you can't take I away from C to make 99 or X from M to make 990. Before anything permanent, run your number through a converter that validates the form, and decide on the date order (day first or month first) yourself.

## The rules in one minute

Roman numerals use seven letters:

| I   | V   | X   | L   | C   | D   | M    |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ---- |
| 1   | 5   | 10  | 50  | 100 | 500 | 1000 |

Standard form follows four rules:

1. **Write from largest to smallest and add.** VIII = 5 + 3 = 8. MMXXVI = 1000 + 1000 + 10 + 10 + 5 + 1 = 2026.
2. **Repeat I, X, C and M at most three times.** 3 is III, but 4 is IV, not IIII.
3. **Never repeat V, L or D.** VV isn't 10, it's X.
4. **Subtract only in the six standard pairs.** A smaller numeral placed before a larger one is subtracted, but only I before V or X, X before L or C, and C before D or M. And only one numeral can be subtracted at a time.

Rule 4 is where nearly every error happens.

## Six numbers people get wrong

| Number | Wrong | Right | Why                                                |
| ------ | ----- | ----- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| 4      | IIII  | IV    | Four repeats aren't allowed                        |
| 49     | IL    | XLIX  | I can only be subtracted from V or X. Write 40 + 9 |
| 99     | IC    | XCIX  | Write 90 + 9                                       |
| 990    | XM    | CMXC  | Write 900 + 90                                     |
| 1990   | MXM   | MCMXC | 1000 + 900 + 90                                    |
| 8      | IIX   | VIII  | You can't subtract two numerals                    |

The trick that fixes most of these is to break the number into its decimal places and convert each one separately. For 1999: 1000 = M, 900 = CM, 90 = XC, 9 = IX, giving **MCMXCIX**. For 1984: M + CM + LXXX + IV = **MCMLXXXIV**.

## Are clock faces wrong, then?

Not exactly. Many clocks use IIII for four, a long-standing design choice that balances the dial visually against VIII on the other side. You'll also see unusual forms on old buildings and manuscripts, because the rules weren't fully standardized in Roman times. But for anything people will read and check today, including tattoos, rings, cornerstones and book copyright dates, use the standard form. Someone will notice.

## How should you write a date?

Convert the day, month and year separately and join them with dots (or dashes, or spaces). The year is the long part. Months and days are short.

The order is a choice you need to make on purpose:

| Date              | Day.Month.Year   | Month.Day.Year (US) |
| ----------------- | ---------------- | ------------------- |
| October 7, 2026   | VII.X.MMXXVI     | X.VII.MMXXVI        |
| June 14, 2025     | XIV.VI.MMXXV     | VI.XIV.MMXXV        |
| December 31, 1999 | XXXI.XII.MCMXCIX | XII.XXXI.MCMXCIX    |

Notice that the first row is ambiguous: VII.X could be July 10 or October 7, depending on the convention the reader assumes. If the date matters, consider writing the month as a word ("7 October MMXXVI") or adding a small note on the order for whoever does the engraving.

## Limits worth knowing

- **There's no zero.** Romans didn't write zero as a numeral, and the converter starts at 1.
- **The standard range ends at 3999 (MMMCMXCIX).** Larger numbers used a bar over the letters to multiply by 1,000, but there's no single agreed way to type that, so converters stop at 3999.
- **No fractions or negatives.** Roman numerals are whole, positive numbers.

## FAQ

### Why does the converter reject my numeral?

You've probably used a non-standard subtraction or too many repeats. VX looks like "5 before 10", but it isn't a valid way to write 5, and XXXX should be XL. The converter flags these and suggests the standard form instead of silently accepting them.

### Should the year in a date be written in full?

Yes. Abbreviating 2026 to XXVI (for '26) is possible, but readers will take it as the number 26. Most people write the full MMXXVI.

### How do I write 2000?

MM. Every year from 2000 to 2099 starts with MM.
