Frequency is how many times something repeats per second, and **1 hertz (Hz) means once per second**. The metric prefixes work as usual: 1 kHz = 1,000 Hz, 1 MHz = 1,000,000 Hz, 1 GHz = 1,000,000,000 Hz. Rotating machines use **revolutions per minute (rpm)**, and because a minute has 60 seconds, **60 rpm = 1 Hz**. A hard drive spinning at 7,200 rpm turns 120 times a second.

Below is what those numbers mean in the devices around you, plus a couple of related calculations a unit converter alone won't do.

## The units at a glance

| Unit | Cycles per second | Typical use                         |
| ---- | ----------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| rpm  | 1/60              | Engines, motors, drives, turntables |
| Hz   | 1                 | Mains power, audio, display refresh |
| kHz  | 1,000             | Audio, AM radio                     |
| MHz  | 1,000,000         | FM radio, older processors          |
| GHz  | 1,000,000,000     | Wi-Fi, mobile networks, modern CPUs |

## Rotation: rpm and Hz

To convert rpm to Hz, divide by 60. To go from Hz to rpm, multiply by 60.

- A 7,200 rpm hard drive: **120 Hz**. A 5,400 rpm laptop drive: **90 Hz**.
- An engine idling at 1,000 rpm: about **16.67 Hz**. At 3,000 rpm: **50 Hz**.
- A vinyl LP at 33⅓ rpm: about **0.556 Hz**, just over half a turn per second.

Mains electricity runs at **50 Hz** in most of the world and **60 Hz** in North America and some other countries. That is 3,000 and 3,600 cycles per minute, which is why simple two-pole synchronous motors connected to the grid spin at exactly those speeds.

## Sound and audio

Musical pitch is frequency. The note A above middle C is tuned to **440 Hz**, or 0.44 kHz. Human hearing spans roughly 20 Hz to 20 kHz, and the upper limit drops with age.

## Radio, Wi-Fi and processors

- **AM radio:** a station at 1,440 kHz is at **1.44 MHz**.
- **FM radio:** 98.5 MHz is **98,500 kHz**.
- **Wi-Fi:** the 2.4 GHz band is **2,400 MHz**; there are also 5 GHz and 6 GHz bands.
- **CPUs:** a processor running at 3.5 GHz is at **3,500 MHz**: 3.5 billion clock cycles per second.

Clock speed alone doesn't decide how fast a processor is, because different chips do different amounts of work per cycle.

## Displays and refresh rates

Monitor refresh rates are frequencies too. A 144 Hz display redraws the image 144 times per second (0.144 kHz). That leads to the most useful related calculation: the **period**.

## Beyond conversion: period and wavelength

Two calculations come up constantly alongside frequency.

**Period = 1 ÷ frequency.** It tells you how long one cycle lasts.

- 50 Hz mains: 1 ÷ 50 = **20 ms** per cycle.
- 144 Hz monitor: 1 ÷ 144 ≈ **6.94 ms** per frame.

**Wavelength = speed ÷ frequency.** For radio waves, the speed is the speed of light, 299,792,458 m/s.

- 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi: 299,792,458 ÷ 2,400,000,000 ≈ **12.5 cm**.
- 98.5 MHz FM: ≈ **3.04 m**, which is why FM antennas are much longer than Wi-Fi ones.

## FAQ

### How many Hz is 1 GHz?

1,000,000,000 Hz, one billion cycles per second.

### Is rpm a frequency?

Yes. It counts full rotations per minute, so it is a frequency with minutes instead of seconds as the time base.

### Why do some countries use 50 Hz and others 60 Hz?

Different early electrical companies chose different standards, and the choices stuck as national grids were built. Both work fine; appliances are designed for one or the other, or both.
