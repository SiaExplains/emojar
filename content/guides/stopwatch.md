A **lap time** is how long one segment took, measured from the previous lap. A **split time** is the total elapsed time from the start to that moment. If you press Lap at 1:52 and again at 3:47, your second lap took 1:55, but your split is 3:47. A good stopwatch records both, so you can see your pace per segment and your overall progress at a glance.

## A worked example: four laps of a track

Say you run four laps of a 400 m track and tap **Lap** each time you pass the start line. The stopwatch records:

| Lap | Lap time | Split    |
| --- | -------- | -------- |
| 1   | 01:52.40 | 01:52.40 |
| 2   | 01:54.70 | 03:47.10 |
| 3   | 01:57.75 | 05:44.85 |
| 4   | 01:53.35 | 07:38.20 |

The split column tells you the whole story of the run: 1,600 m in 7 minutes 38.2 seconds. The lap column tells you what happened inside it. Lap 1 was the fastest, lap 3 was the slowest, and you picked up the pace again on the last lap, a typical pattern for someone who starts a little too quickly.

For the first lap, lap time and split are always the same, since both are measured from the start.

## Turning laps into pace

A few quick calculations make lap data much more useful:

- **Average lap:** total time ÷ laps. 458.2 s ÷ 4 = 114.55 s, or **1:54.55** per lap.
- **Pace per kilometer:** 458.2 s for 1.6 km is 286.4 s per km, about **4:46 per km**.
- **Consistency:** the gap between fastest and slowest lap is 5.35 seconds. Bringing that gap down is often a better training goal than raw speed.

## Other things worth timing with laps

Laps aren't only for running. Anything with repeated segments benefits:

- **Swimming lengths and cycling intervals.** Tap at each wall or marker.
- **Presentations and speeches.** Tap at each section change to see which part runs long.
- **Cooking and brewing steps.** Tap at each stage to repeat a recipe exactly next time.
- **Routine tasks.** Timing each step of a weekly report, for example, shows where the time actually goes.
- **Practice drills.** Musicians and speakers can time repetitions of the same passage to track progress.

## How accurate is an online stopwatch?

The display shows hundredths of a second, but the real limit is usually your finger. Human reaction time to a visual signal is typically around 0.2 seconds, and it varies from press to press. For hand timing, trust tenths more than hundredths, and use the same person and the same cue (a foot crossing the line, a word spoken) for every lap.

What you _can_ rely on is that the stopwatch itself doesn't drift. Some browser timers count "ticks", which browsers slow down when a tab is in the background. The [Online Stopwatch](/online-tool/date-time-tools/stopwatch) instead measures elapsed time from your device's high-resolution clock each time it updates the display. If you switch to another tab or app for ten minutes, the time is still exact when you come back. The elapsed time also appears in the browser tab's title while it runs, so you can check it from another tab. Closing the page does stop it, though.

## Using the stopwatch step by step

1. Press **Start**, or the space bar.
2. Press **Lap**, or the **L** key, at the end of each segment. The table fills in with **Lap time** and **Split** for every lap, newest at the top.
3. Once you have two or more laps, the **fastest** lap is marked in green and the **slowest** in red.
4. Press **Stop** (or space) to pause. **Resume** continues from the same time.
5. When stopped, press **Reset**, or **R**, to clear the time and laps.

Times under an hour show as minutes, seconds and hundredths (`07:38.20`). Past an hour, hours appear in front (`1:02:05.12`).

## Tips for cleaner data

- **Decide your lap cue before starting.** "When my foot hits the line" beats "roughly at the corner".
- **Use the keyboard.** A key press is quicker and more consistent than moving a mouse to a button. On a phone, keep your thumb resting on the button.
- **Write down the lap table before you reset.** Reset clears everything, and the data isn't saved.
- **Don't press Reset to start a new lap.** That clears the session. Use Lap instead.

## FAQ

### Can I count down instead of up?

A stopwatch only counts up. For a countdown with an alarm, use the [Online Timer](/online-tool/date-time-tools/online-timer).

### Why can't I press Reset while it's running?

The second button becomes **Lap** while the stopwatch runs, so you can't wipe a session by accident. Stop it first, then reset.

### Does it work on a phone?

Yes. The buttons work by touch. Keep the page open while you time: if the phone's browser unloads the page in the background to save memory, the session is lost, just as it is when you close the tab.
