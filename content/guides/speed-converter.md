**100 km/h is about 62.1 mph**, and **60 mph is about 96.6 km/h**. To go from meters per second to kilometers per hour, multiply by **3.6**. One knot is one nautical mile per hour, exactly **1.852 km/h**. Which unit you need depends on the context: roads, water, air and science each have their own convention.

## Which speed unit is used where?

| Context                            | Usual unit | Why                                          |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Road signs, most countries         | km/h       | Metric distances on signs                    |
| Road signs in the US and UK        | mph        | Distances in miles                           |
| Ships, aircraft, wind at sea       | knots (kn) | Tied to nautical miles and navigation charts |
| Physics, engineering, weather data | m/s        | The SI unit of speed                         |
| Ballistics, sports science (US)    | ft/s       | Imperial engineering tradition               |
| Fast aircraft                      | Mach       | Speed relative to the speed of sound         |

## How do the conversions work?

Every speed unit is a distance divided by a time, so each conversion combines a length factor and a time factor.

- **m/s to km/h:** 1 m/s is 3,600 m per hour, or 3.6 km/h. So 10 m/s = **36 km/h**, and 12 km/h = **3.33 m/s**.
- **km/h to mph:** divide by 1.609344 (the kilometers in a mile). 100 km/h = **62.14 mph**.
- **Knots:** a nautical mile is 1,852 m, so 1 kn = 1.852 km/h = **1.1508 mph**.
- **ft/s to mph:** 88 ft/s is exactly **60 mph**, a handy reference point (5,280 ft × 60 ÷ 3,600 = 88).

## Mental shortcuts for the road

- **km/h to mph:** multiply by 0.6 and add a little. 50 km/h × 0.6 = 30, actual **31.07 mph**.
- **mph to km/h:** multiply by 1.6. 70 mph × 1.6 = 112, actual **112.65 km/h**.

## Knots on the water and in the air

Knots make navigation easy: one nautical mile was originally one minute of latitude, so speed and position on a chart share a unit. A sailing boat doing 25 knots is moving at **12.86 m/s**. A jet cruising at 450 knots is doing **833.4 km/h** relative to the air around it; ground speed differs with the wind.

## What is Mach, exactly?

Mach is speed divided by the local speed of sound. Because the speed of sound depends on air temperature, Mach 1 is not a fixed number of km/h. The converter uses the standard sea-level value of **340.29 m/s**, which makes Mach 1 = **1,225 km/h** or **761.2 mph**. At cruising altitude, where the air is much colder, Mach 1 is noticeably slower, so treat Mach conversions here as a sea-level reference.

Runners often use pace, minutes per kilometer or per mile, which is time divided by distance, the inverse of speed. ## FAQ

### Is a knot the same as a mile per hour?

No. A knot is a nautical mile per hour, about 15% faster than 1 mph.

### Why is m/s used in weather data?

It is the SI unit, so scientific models and many weather services use it. To get a feel for wind speeds, multiply by 3.6 for km/h: a 10 m/s wind is 36 km/h.

### How fast is the speed of light in these units?

Exactly 299,792,458 m/s, which is about 1,079,252,849 km/h or roughly 670.6 million mph. Enter it in m/s to see it in every unit.
