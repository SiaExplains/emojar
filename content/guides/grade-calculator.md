To find the score you need on a final exam, take your target course grade, subtract the part of it already locked in by your current grade, and divide by the final's weight. As a formula: **needed = (target − current × (1 − final weight)) ÷ final weight**, with the weight written as a decimal. The rest of this post walks through one student's gradebook, step by step, so you can do the same with yours.

## The situation

Maya is two weeks from her chemistry final. The syllabus says:

| Category   | Weight | Maya's score |
| ---------- | ------ | ------------ |
| Homework   | 20%    | 92%          |
| Quizzes    | 15%    | 85%          |
| Midterm    | 25%    | 78%          |
| Final exam | 40%    | ?            |

She wants to know three things: where she stands now, what she needs for a B+ (87%), and whether an A- (90%) is still possible.

## Step 1: Work out the current weighted grade

A weighted grade multiplies each score by its weight, adds those up, and divides by the total weight graded so far.

- Homework: 92 × 20 = 1,840
- Quizzes: 85 × 15 = 1,275
- Midterm: 78 × 25 = 1,950
- Total: 5,065 ÷ 60 = **84.4%**

Dividing by 60, not 100, is the important part. Only 60% of the course has been graded, so her current grade describes her performance on that 60%. This is how most online gradebooks display a running grade. On a standard scale, 84.4% is a **B**.

## Step 2: Find the final exam score for each target

With a 40% final, the other 60% of the course is fixed at 84.4%. That portion contributes 84.4 × 0.6 = 50.65 points to the final grade. The exam has to supply the rest:

| Target   | Calculation        | Needed on final |
| -------- | ------------------ | --------------- |
| 70% (C-) | (70 − 50.65) ÷ 0.4 | 48.4%           |
| 80% (B-) | (80 − 50.65) ÷ 0.4 | 73.4%           |
| 85% (B)  | (85 − 50.65) ÷ 0.4 | 85.9%           |
| 87% (B+) | (87 − 50.65) ÷ 0.4 | 90.9%           |
| 90% (A-) | (90 − 50.65) ÷ 0.4 | 98.4%           |

So the A- is technically alive but needs a near-perfect exam. The B+ needs about 91%.

## How much does the final's weight change things?

A lot. Here's a different student, with 83.8% across 75% of the course and a final worth only 25%:

- To finish with 80%, they need **68.6%** on the final.
- To finish with 90%, they would need **108.6%**, which isn't possible without extra credit.
- Even a 28.6% keeps them at 70%.

A small final means your semester work has mostly decided the outcome, for better or worse. A heavy final (40–50%) leaves much more room to move up or down.

## Mistakes that throw the number off

- **Entering points instead of percentages.** A quiz score of 17/20 should go in as 85, not 17.
- **Using category totals from the wrong place.** If your gradebook already shows "Homework: 92%", enter that once with the full category weight, rather than every assignment with a made-up share.
- **Forgetting dropped scores.** Many courses drop the lowest quiz. Check what your gradebook actually counts.
- **Assuming the letter cutoffs.** The letter shown uses a common scale (93 for an A, 90 for an A-, 87 for a B+, and so on). Your syllabus may round up from 89.5 or use different cutoffs.
- **Points-based courses.** If your class adds up raw points rather than weighted categories, each item's weight is its share of total points. A 200-point final in a 1,000-point course has a 20% weight.

## When the answer looks bad

A required score of 95% or more is a signal to have a conversation, not to panic. Ask whether there's extra credit, whether the final can replace a lower midterm, or whether a grade is still missing from the gradebook. If the target is truly out of reach, recalculating with a lower target tells you what score keeps you safely above a cutoff that matters, such as a C for prerequisites.

## FAQ

### Can I use this for a course that has more than one exam left?

Yes, roughly. Combine the remaining exams into one "final" with their total weight, and the needed score is the average you'd need across them.

### Why does my teacher's grade differ slightly?

Rounding and category rules. Some instructors round each category before weighting, which can shift the result by a few tenths of a percent.

### Does a higher target always require a proportionally higher final?

Yes. With a 40% final, every extra point of target needs 2.5 more points on the exam (1 ÷ 0.4). With a 25% final, it's 4 points.
