A good profile picture is a tight, well-lit crop of your face, centered so a circle can't cut off anything important, and exported at around 800 × 800 pixels. Add a border only if it helps you stand out, keep it thin, and save round pictures as PNG so the corners stay transparent. The checklist below walks through each decision with the settings to use.

## Why do so many profile pictures fail?

Because they're designed at full screen and seen at thumbnail size. Your picture shows up next to comments, in chat lists and in search results, often not much bigger than a fingernail. A photo that looks great at full size, say you on a mountain with the view behind you, turns into a few colored pixels where your face should be. That's why Emojar's Profile Picture Maker includes a "How it looks small" preview about 40 pixels across. If you can't recognize yourself there, nobody else will.

## The checklist

### 1. Start with a sharp source photo

Pick a photo that's in focus, evenly lit and shows your face clearly. Resolution matters more than people expect. If your face covers only a 300-pixel square in the original and you export at 1024 × 1024, the tool has to stretch those 300 pixels to more than three times their size, and the result looks soft. A recent phone photo taken from a normal distance gives plenty of detail.

### 2. Fill about two thirds of the frame with your face

Use the **Zoom** slider (100% to 500%) and drag the photo until your face takes up roughly two thirds of the shape. Tight crops read better small. Leave a little space above your hair so the top of your head isn't cut off.

### 3. Center for the circle, not the square

A circle inside a square covers π/4 of its area, about 78.5%. In other words, around 21% of a square photo, all of it in the corners, disappears when a platform shows it as a circle. Most networks crop to a circle on their own, so even if you choose a square, keep everything important away from the corners. The editor darkens the area outside the shape so you can see exactly what gets cut.

### 4. Choose the shape on purpose

The **Shape** menu has three options:

| Shape          | Good for                                                   |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Circle         | Social profiles, so you see the final crop in advance      |
| Rounded square | Workspace or community icons that display with soft corners |
| Square         | Platforms that show the full square, or a JPG with no corners |

The rounded square uses a corner radius of 18% of the picture's width.

### 5. Keep the border thin, or skip it

Under **Border** you can pick **No border**, **Solid colour** or **Gradient ring**. The gradients are Sunset (yellow to pink to purple), Ocean (cyan to blue), Forest (lime to teal) and Monochrome. **Border width** runs from 1% to 15% of the picture size and starts at 4%.

Here's what that means in pixels. At 800 × 800 px with a 4% border, the ring is 32 px wide on each side, so your photo fills the inner 736 × 736 px. At 15%, the ring takes 120 px per side and the photo shrinks to 560 px. Thick rings eat into your face, so 3–6% is usually plenty. A colored ring helps you stand out in a busy feed; in more formal settings, a clean photo without a border often looks better.

### 6. Pick PNG or JPG deliberately

**Transparent background (PNG)** is on by default. It keeps everything outside the shape invisible, so a round picture blends into any background, light or dark.

Switch it off and the download becomes a JPG, with a **Background** color painted behind your photo inside the shape. That color only shows where the photo itself is transparent, for example a cutout made with the [Image Background Remover](/online-tool/image-tools/image-background-remover). JPG has no transparency, so the corners outside a circle or rounded square are filled with the same background color. Pick a color that matches where the picture will appear, or keep the PNG if you're not sure.

### 7. Export at the right size

The **Size** menu offers 400 × 400 px, 800 × 800 px (recommended) and 1024 × 1024 px. The 800 px option is large enough for any major platform to scale down cleanly. Use 1024 px only when your source photo has the detail to back it up (see point 1).

### 8. Check the small preview one last time

Glance at the tiny preview next to the large one. Can you tell it's you? Does the ring overpower the face? Make adjustments now rather than after you've uploaded.

## FAQ

### Why does my picture look blurry after I upload it?

Usually the source was too small for the export size, or the crop zoomed into a small part of a low-resolution photo. Start from a sharper, larger original, export at 800 px, and avoid zooming in so far that the face comes from only a few hundred pixels.

### Can I use it for a logo instead of a face?

Yes. Any image works, and a transparent PNG logo inside a gradient ring makes a tidy community or channel icon. For a website's browser-tab icon, the [Favicon Generator](/online-tool/image-tools/favicon-generator) produces the specific sizes browsers need.

### Should I use the same picture on every platform?

It helps people recognize you across sites. One 800 × 800 px PNG with your face centered works almost everywhere, because each platform scales and crops it for its own layout.
