To turn a yearly salary into an hourly rate, divide it by the hours you are actually paid for in a year: hours per week times paid weeks per year. At 40 hours and 52 weeks that is 2,080 hours, so $60,000 works out to $28.85 an hour. Change either number and the answer moves, which is where most quick mental math goes wrong.

Here are five beliefs that lead to the wrong figure, each checked with the Salary to Hourly Calculator. All results are gross pay estimates, before tax and deductions, and not financial or tax advice.

## Myth 1: Every salary divides by 2,080

The 2,080 shortcut assumes a 40-hour week. Plenty of contracts specify 37.5 or 35 hours, and that changes the hourly value of the same salary.

| Salary  | Hours per week | Paid weeks | Hourly rate |
| ------- | -------------- | ---------- | ----------- |
| 60,000  | 40             | 52         | 28.85       |
| 60,000  | 37.5           | 52         | 30.77       |

Two and a half fewer hours a week raise the hourly rate by almost $2. If you are comparing two job offers with different standard weeks, compare them per hour, not per year.

## Myth 2: A month is four weeks of pay

It is tempting to multiply weekly pay by four. On a $60,000 salary, weekly pay is 1,153.85, and four of those make 4,615.40. The calculator's **Monthly** figure is 5,000.00, because it divides the yearly amount by 12, not by 4. A year has 52 weeks, which is about 4.33 per month, so the four-week shortcut understates monthly income by close to 8%.

The reverse mistake matters too. If a landlord or lender asks for monthly income and you are paid weekly, multiplying by four makes your income look smaller than it is.

## Myth 3: Bi-weekly and semi-monthly paychecks are the same

They sound alike but produce different paychecks. On $60,000 the **Pay for every period** table shows:

- **Semi-monthly** (twice a month, 24 paychecks): 2,500.00
- **Bi-weekly** (every two weeks, 26 paychecks): 2,307.69

The yearly total is identical. The bi-weekly check is smaller, but two months a year contain a third payday. Budgeting on the smaller figure and treating those extra checks as a bonus is a common, simple approach.

## Myth 4: Your salary's hourly rate is a fair freelance rate

Suppose you leave a $60,000 job to contract. Employees are usually paid for holidays and vacation; contractors are not. If you expect six weeks a year without paid work, set **Paid weeks per year** to 46:

- To earn 60,000 over 46 paid weeks at 40 hours, you need **32.61 an hour**, not 28.85.
- Charging $30 an hour over those same 46 weeks brings in **55,200.00** a year.

And that is before anything a salary package might have covered, such as health insurance, retirement contributions or equipment. The calculator handles the time side of the comparison; you need to add the cost side yourself.

## Myth 5: The hourly figure is what you take home

Every number the tool produces is gross pay. Income tax, social contributions and benefit deductions come out afterwards, and they depend on where you live and your circumstances. Use the results to compare offers on equal terms, then look up net pay separately.

## FAQ

### Why doesn't my monthly pay change when I lower the paid weeks?

If you enter a yearly salary, the year's total stays fixed and the monthly figure is that total divided by 12. Fewer paid weeks change the hourly, daily and weekly figures instead, because the same money is spread over fewer working weeks.

### How do I convert part-time pay?

Enter the salary you are actually paid and your real hours. A 30,000 salary for 20 hours a week over 52 weeks is the same 28.85 an hour as 60,000 at 40 hours.

### What is the highest number of hours I can enter?

Up to 168 hours per week, the number of hours in a week, with up to 7 days per week and 53 weeks per year. Values outside those ranges show an error.
