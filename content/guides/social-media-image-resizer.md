Every social network has a preferred shape for each kind of image, and when your picture doesn't match, the platform crops it for you, often through a face or a line of text. The fix is to resize to the exact size before uploading and choose for yourself what stays visible. The main decision is whether to **crop** (fill the frame and lose some edges) or **fit** (keep the whole picture and fill the leftover space with a background).

This guide covers both: the sizes, then how to choose.

## Crop or fit: how much of your photo survives?

The answer depends on how far apart the two shapes are. Take a typical 4000 × 3000 phone photo (4:3, landscape):

| Target                         | Crop keeps                    | Fit leaves                              |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| YouTube thumbnail, 1280 × 720  | 4000 × 2250 (75% of the height) | the photo at 960 × 720, bars on the sides |
| Instagram portrait, 1080 × 1350 | 2400 × 3000 (60% of the width) | the photo at 1080 × 810, bands above and below |
| Instagram story, 1080 × 1920   | 1688 × 3000 (42% of the width) | the photo at 1080 × 810, 555 px bands above and below |
| LinkedIn background, 1584 × 396 | 4000 × 1000 (a third of the height) | the photo at 528 × 396 in the middle |

The pattern: when the shapes are close, cropping costs little and looks best. When they're far apart, cropping throws away most of the picture, and fitting shrinks it to a small island in the middle.

## Which mode should you choose?

The **How to fit** setting has three options:

- **Crop to fill (drag to position).** The image covers the whole frame. Drag it to choose which part shows, and use **Zoom** (100% to 400%) to crop in tighter. Best when the shapes are similar, or when the subject is compact, such as a face or a product.
- **Fit whole image, blurred background.** The full picture sits in the middle on a soft, slightly darkened, enlarged copy of itself. Best for stories and Shorts made from landscape photos: the frame feels full, and nothing important is cut off.
- **Fit whole image, solid background.** The same, but on a plain color you pick under **Background**. Best for graphics, screenshots, charts and quotes, where a clean border looks deliberate and a blur would look odd.

A rough rule: if cropping would keep more than about two-thirds of the image, crop. If it would keep less than half, fit. In between, look at where the subject sits and decide.

1. Open the [Social Media Image Resizer](/online-tool/image-tools/social-media-image-resizer) and pick a **Platform** (the single-network pages skip this step), then a **Size**.

5. Or click the "All … sizes (ZIP)" button to export every size for that platform at once.

## Watch out for these

- **"All sizes" uses one focus point.** In crop mode, the ZIP applies the same position and zoom to every size. Check the extreme shapes, like a header or banner, separately.
- **Small images are scaled up.** A 600-pixel image resized to 1080 wide will be enlarged and look soft. Start from the original photo, not a screenshot of it.
- **Banners hide behind profile photos.** On profile headers and backgrounds, keep faces and text away from the edges where profile pictures and buttons tend to overlap.

## FAQ

### Should I upload exactly these sizes, or larger?

Exactly these sizes are a safe choice. A larger image of the same shape usually works too, but uploading at the platform's own size means one less resize on their end.

### Can I make a profile picture round here?

The profile presets produce a square image, and networks apply the circle themselves. To crop into a circle and add a colored or gradient border, use the [Profile Picture Maker](/online-tool/image-tools/profile-picture-maker).
